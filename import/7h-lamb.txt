7h lamb (8 ppl)

Ingredients
- Leg of lamb, about 2 kg, bone-in
- 4 carrots, peeled and cut into large pieces
- 4 onions, cut into quarters
- 200 g dried tomatoes
- 400 g peeled, seeded tomatoes, roughly chopped
- 200 g pork rind (couenne)
- 25 cl beef stock
- 40 cl dry white wine
- 8 cl cognac
- 8 cl olive oil
- 1 bouquet garni (thyme, bay leaf, parsley, and 1–2 cloves stuck in a piece of onion, or similar)
- Fine salt
- Freshly ground black pepper

1) Dry-brine the lamb (ideally the day before)
Salt the leg of lamb generously all over. Place it in the fridge, loosely covered or uncovered, for at least 1 hour and up to 24 hours.

2) Sear the lamb
- Preheat the oven to 120–130°C.
- Pat the lamb dry if needed, season with pepper.
- Heat the olive oil in the cocotte over medium-high heat.
- Sear the lamb on all sides until well browned, then remove and set aside.

3) Build the base with pork rind, vegetables and liquids
- Sweat the onions and carrots in the same cocotte
- Deglaze the pan with the cognac
- Add the white wine, beef stock, tomatoes, dried tomatoes, and bouquet garni.
- Season lightly with salt and pepper, bring to a gentle simmer.
- Place the seared lamb on top of the vegetables and rind.
- Adjust liquid so it comes about two-thirds up the side of the meat

4) Long, slow braise
- Cover with the lid and transfer to the oven.
- Cook 6–7 hours at 120–130°C.
- The braising liquid should stay at a gentle simmer, not a hard boil.

5) Check doneness
- From about 5–6 hours, check with a fork or skewer in the thickest part: it should slide in very easily and the meat should almost fall apart.
- If still resistant, continue cooking and check again after 30–45 minutes.

6) Optional final browning
- If you want a deeper crust: remove the lid, raise oven to 180–200°C.
- Roast 15–30 minutes, basting once or twice, until nicely browned.

7) Finish and serve
- Transfer the lamb to a warm dish to rest, loosely covered.
- If needed, reduce the sauce on the stove with the lid off until it's slightly thickened and flavourful; skim excess fat.
- Adjust seasoning with salt and pepper.
- Serve the lamb very tender, directly from the cocotte or on a platter, with the vegetables and plenty of sauce. It should be soft enough to portion with a spoon.
