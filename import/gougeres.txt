Gougères (thermomix-first, no-collapse)

Yields
~28–32 bite-size gougères (4 cm piped mounds)

Ingredients (all weighed)
- 120 g water
- 120 g whole milk
- 100 g unsalted butter
- 1 tsp fine salt
- 1 g freshly ground black pepper
- 1–2 pinches ground nutmeg or Espelette
- 160 g plain (all-purpose) flour
- 200 g finely grated cheese (mix is best): 130 g Comté or Gruyère (flavor + melt) + 70 g Parmesan (structure + umami)
- 4 large eggs

1) Prep & preheat
1. Place racks in upper-middle and lower-middle.
2. Preheat to 210 °C conventional. High initial heat = rapid steam = lift.
3. Grate 200 g cheese

2) Make the panade
1. Into the bowl: water 120 g, milk 120 g, butter 100 g, 1 tsp salt, pepper, nutmeg. Heat 5 min / 98 °C / speed 1 (MC on) until fully melted and just bubbling.
2. Add flour 160 g. Mix 20 sec / speed 4 to combine.
3. Dry the dough: Cook 3 min / 100 °C / speed 3 (MC off, basket on lid to prevent splatter). You want a smooth ball pulling from the sides — a thin film on the bowl is good (means moisture cooked off).

3) Cool slightly before eggs
- Mix 1 min / speed 3 (no heat) to drop temp; aim for ~60 °C or cooler so eggs don't scramble.

4) Add eggs to texture, not just a number
1. Set speed 4, add beaten eggs through the MC opening in 3–4 additions over ~1½–2 min.
2. After 220 g, pause and check: lift the spatula — dough should stretch into a V-shaped ribbon that slowly breaks. If it snaps stiffly, add more egg 10–15 g at a time, briefly mixing after each.
3. You want glossy, pipeable, holds ridges but softly — like thick mayonnaise. Too stiff = no rise; too loose = pancakes.

5) Add cheese
- Add 200 g grated cheese. Mix 15–20 sec / speed 3.5 — just to distribute; don't overwork.

6) Finish
1. Use spoons to create 3.5–4 cm mounds, spaced 4–5 cm. (Two spoons also fine.)
2. Tidy tops: finger dipped in water to smooth points (prevents burning).
3. Optional: sprinkle a pinch of Parmesan or flaky salt on each.

7) Bake smart (no peeking!)
1. Bake at 210 °C for 10 minutes to inflate.
2. Without opening the door, reduce to 185 °C and bake 10 minutes.
3. Quickly open the oven once at minute ~20 to vent steam (2–3 seconds), then finish baking to color (5-8 more minutes).

8) Set the structure
- Remove trays; pierce each puff on the side or bottom with a skewer to release steam.
- Return to the off oven with door ajar for 5 minutes to dry (best texture, longer lasting).

Serving & storage
- Serve warm as is, or split and fill (bresaola + crème fraîche, chive omelette folds, etc.).
- Hold: low oven ~80–90 °C for up to 45 min.
- Freeze: fully baked and cooled puffs in a bag up to 1 month. Recrisp 180 °C for 6–8 min from frozen.

Common pitfalls (and fixes)
- Flat / dense → panade not dried enough or too little egg: fully cook the panade; add egg to the V-ribbon texture.
- Spread too much → dough too loose: next time stop eggs earlier; add 1–2 tsp flour and mix briefly (emergency fix).
- Collapse after baking → underbaked/pale or trapped steam: bake to deep golden; prick and dry 5 min in ajar oven.
- Cheese blobs leaking → grate very fine; fold in briefly; keep pieces small.

Flavor upgrades (keep structure intact)
- Gruyère–parmesan–pepper (as above): classic, super savory.
- Comté + smoked paprika (½ tsp) for warmth.
- Chèvre (fresh goat's cheese 60 g) + herbs: replace part of Gruyère; add 2 tsp chopped chives.
- Mustard: whisk 1–2 tsp Dijon into the beaten eggs before adding.

Quick success checklist
- panade leaves a film on bowl = dry enough
- dough temp < ~60 °C before eggs
- V-ribbon texture after eggs
- hot start, then lower temp; don't open early
- prick & dry to finish
